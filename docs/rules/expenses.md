## Policy

### Authority to spend

- Every purchase is made under the board-approved budget or a board rule, and
  follows the Procurement Policy. — *Financial Policy, Art. VII §VII.2*

- Nobody commits the Treehouse to spending, orally or in writing, without
  approval, and nobody signs an agreement or lease on its behalf; agreements go
  to the board. — *Fundraising, Financial Commitments and Approvals Policy,
  Arts. III–IV*

- A program commits no spending until the board has approved its program
  budget. Spending incurred earlier needs board approval, and a leader who
  incurs it does so at their own risk. — *Sponsored Program Policy, Art. III
  §III.6*

- A program leader may designate program treasurers in the board-approved
  program budget, and they approve expenses on the leader's behalf. That
  approval confirms the expense is within the program budget; it is not the
  full approval to reimburse. — *Sponsored Program Policy, Art. VI §VI.1*

- Program spending is reimbursed only within the capacity of the program
  budget. A program that does not cover its required spending may be refused
  reimbursement. — *Sponsored Program Policy, Art. VI §§VI.2–VI.3*

- A purchase of $2,000 or more is reviewed by the board directly. One under
  $2,000 in a program budget is reviewed by the Treasurer and the program's
  leader or treasurer. — *Procurement Policy, Art. III*

- The same person does not authorise, execute and monitor a transaction.
  — *Financial Policy, Art. III*

### What may not be bought

- No personal transaction goes through the Treehouse or a Treehouse Card. Nor
  do cash advances, loans, payroll advances, purchases for other organisations,
  alcohol, personal entertainment, fuel for personal vehicles, or anything
  inconsistent with the mission. — *Financial Policy, Art. IV*

- A purchase from a business owned or operated by an individual needs the
  board's approval in advance. — *Financial Policy, Art. IV*

- Food and drink go only to participating students and Treehouse Volunteers
  unless the board approves otherwise in writing in advance, and a meal for
  volunteers is for the Treehouse's convenience, such as keeping them on site
  during a program. — *Sponsored Program Policy, Art. VI §VI.2; Travel Policy,
  Art. XI; Anti-bribery and Gift Acceptance Policy, Art. VII*

- A gift to a supplier, sponsor or partner is at most $50, at most once every
  six months per recipient, and never cash or a gift card. Any gift to a
  government official needs the board's approval first. — *Anti-bribery and
  Gift Acceptance Policy, Art. IV; Art. V §V.2*

### Everyday purchases and procurement

- A purchase is Everyday when its total is below $500 and no single item is
  over $250. An Everyday purchase needs no written record of price-shopping.
  — *Procurement Policy, Art. IV §IV.1*

- The Treasurer or the board may withdraw that exemption from a program, a
  program leader's area, or individuals who make unreasonably high-priced
  purchases. — *Procurement Policy, Art. IV §IV.1*

- A purchase from a vendor on the board's written list of approved
  single-source vendors is treated as Everyday for purchase procedures.
  — *Procurement Policy, Art. IV §IV.3*

- A purchase that is not Everyday follows the procurement tier for its size —
  three documented quotes where possible below $2,500, written estimates below
  $10,000, and open competition above — and its records show why it was needed
  and how the method was chosen. — *Procurement Policy, Arts. IV–VII, IX*

### Reimbursement

- Only Treehouse Leaders and Treehouse Volunteers spend on the Treehouse's
  behalf and ask to be reimbursed. Reimbursing someone who is not a member
  needs board approval. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §§IX.1–IX.2*

- A reimbursed expense was authorised by the board or, within a board-approved
  program budget, by the program's leader or treasurer. Approval in advance is
  preferred; approval afterwards is allowed when the expense is consistent with
  policy and the program's needs. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.2*

- Reimbursement is requested within 60 days of the purchase. — *Financial
  Policy, Art. IX §IX.2*

- Every reimbursement needs a receipt showing the date, the amount and a
  description of what was bought; a card slip or store receipt that does not
  describe the purchase is not enough. Without a receipt, the purchaser signs a
  note giving the date, purchaser, vendor, description, amount and payment
  type. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.2*

- A signed note in place of a receipt may be reimbursed below $50, within a
  board-approved program budget, on the approval of the program's leader or
  treasurer and the Treasurer. Above $50, or outside a program budget, it needs
  the board. The Treasurer or the board may withdraw this from a program, a
  program leader's area, or individuals. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §§IX.2,
  IX.4*

- A meal receipt states how many people were fed. Within a program budget, the
  people fed who are not the program's participants or volunteers are named;
  otherwise everyone fed is named. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.2*

- Travel is reimbursed only when it was approved in writing in advance by the
  board or the program's leader or treasurer, and at no more than the IRS
  mileage rate and the IRS meal per diem. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.2*

- Purchases use the Treehouse's tax exemptions. A reimbursement request where
  they were not used explains why, and the Treasurer or the board may refuse to
  reimburse tax where this becomes unreasonable. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX
  §§IX.2, IX.4*

- A reimbursement is signed in writing by the submitter; by the program's
  leader or treasurer, or outside a program budget by a board member who is
  not the Treasurer and has no conflict with the submitter; and by the
  Treasurer. A conflicted Treasurer adds one more board member without a
  conflict, and a Treasurer conflicted on both the submitter's side and the
  program's side adds two. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.3*

- An overpaid reimbursement is returned. — *Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.6*

### Treehouse Cards

- Any card opened on the Treehouse's EIN is a Treehouse Card. The board decides
  who holds one. — *Financial Policy, Art. II; Art. XI §XI.2*

- A Treehouse Card is never used for cash or a cash equivalent, nor for a
  personal purchase even if the holder means to repay it. — *Financial Policy,
  Art. XII §XII.1*

- The board, the Treasurer or the bookkeeper may set limits on a person's
  individual or monthly card spending. — *Financial Policy, Art. XII §XII.1*

- A card charge is reconciled with its original receipt. It is approved by the
  program's leader or treasurer when in a program budget, and by the Treasurer.
  A charge that is not Everyday and was not board-authorised in writing also
  needs a board member without a conflict. When the Treasurer made the
  purchase, another board member without a conflict takes the Treasurer's
  place, and two are needed for a charge that is not Everyday and not
  board-authorised in writing. — *Financial Policy, Art. XII §XII.4*

- A card charge whose receipt is not in within 15 days is paid at the
  Treasurer's discretion if it is Everyday or board-authorised, and otherwise
  only by board action. The purchaser gives the Treasurer a written reason for
  any missing receipt. — *Financial Policy, Art. XII §XII.6*

- Using a personal card where a Treehouse Card was reasonable may lead the
  Treasurer or the board to reduce or refuse reimbursement, 60 days after
  written notice. — *Financial Policy, Art. VIII §VIII.3*

### Records

- Nobody falsifies a budget or financial record. — *Records Policy, Art. III*

- Reimbursement amounts are shared only on a need-to-know basis. — *Records
  Policy, Art. IV*

- Receipts, invoices and the other source documents of a transaction are kept
  as an audit trail: expense and tax records for at least seven years from the
  return's filing, and invoices and payables ledgers for seven years.
  — *Procurement Policy, Art. IX; Records Policy, Art. VI §§VI.4.B, VI.4.J*

- Each expense is classed as management, shared facility or shop cost, a
  program's direct cost, or fundraising. Where that is ambiguous, the Treasurer
  places an item specific to one program in that program's budget, allocates a
  shared one, and otherwise treats it as management. — *Financial Policy,
  Art. XV §XV.2; Finance Structure Framework, §§I–II*

  outside the app. The app does not model notes in lieu of receipts.

## Procedure

### Who may see and do what

- Being on the Board is enough to view receipt and expense data, and to edit it
  where the app allows an edit.
  [Decision — owner]

- An adult member or a staff/role holder may submit a receipt and view, edit,
  and fetch their own receipts. An adult member is a person whose session age
  band is adult and whose household membership is ACTIVE. A role holder or a
  Treehouse staff account is admitted whatever their recorded age or membership.
  A signed-in adult with no ACTIVE membership is refused, and so is a youth or a
  person of unknown age without a role, because a self-declared age alone is not
  proof of adulthood. No background check is needed. The submitter sees only
  their own receipts, without donor or reimbursee names, and approval stays with
  FINANCE, so the audience exposes no other person's data and moves no money on
  its own. The same audience may reach an expense sign-off for the submitter
  seat, since that seat is filled from the receipt. Widening it to any signed-in
  adult, narrowing it to the catalog audience, or adding a background-check leg
  is a decision on the record.
  [Decision — owner]

### Receipts

- Nobody makes a finance decision on a receipt that they or their household
  uploaded or is reimbursed for. Until a receipt is finalized, its submitter
  may correct its tax and date; a receipt whose tax or date its submitter
  corrected goes to finance review.
  [Decision — *Policy: Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

### Budget buckets

- A budget bucket is an accounting bucket, not a person. Finance creates,
  renames and archives buckets and maps each to a class in the accounting
  system. Archiving keeps the row, because finance records point at it.
  [Decision]

- A program bucket's approvers are the program's leader and its treasurers,
  derived and never stored. An org-level bucket has none.
  [Decision]

- A program treasurer is set by the board alone, because the role comes from the
  board-approved program budget. Nobody sets it on themself or anyone in their
  own household. Treasurers are adult-only by corporate policy, and the app does
  not check that.
  [Decision — *Policy: Sponsored Program Policy, Art. VI §VI.1; Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

### Expenses

- Every expense that is not a reimbursement is treated as a Treehouse Card
  charge. The app records no payment method.
  [Decision — owner]

### Spending sign-off

- A reimbursement or card charge reaches the books only once each of its lines
  is signed by the submitter, a program approver, and the Treasurer — three
  different people from three different households.
  [Decision — *Policy: Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.3*]

- "The Treasurer" is, for now, anyone holding the finance role. The app has no
  record of who holds the Treasurer office.
  [Decision — owner]

- A Treasurer with a conflict does not sign. For the Treasurer seat, a conflict
  is being — or sharing a household with — the submitter, the person reimbursed,
  or the line's Program Leader, or having approved the line as its program
  approver. Another finance holder without a conflict signs instead; if there is
  none, a Board member without a conflict takes the seat. This differs from the
  policy on purpose: the policy has the conflicted Treasurer sign and adds one
  or two Board members, where the app replaces the conflicted signer, because
  nobody decides on a receipt they are conflicted on.
  [Decision — *Policy: Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.3; Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

- When every Program Leader and Program Treasurer of a line's program is
  conflicted or unavailable, a Board member without a conflict signs the
  program-approver seat. The policy names no one for this case; the app fills
  the gap rather than leave the line held for good.
  [Decision — owner]

- Reimbursing someone who is not a Treehouse Member takes a Board member in the
  program-approver seat. What counts is the person reimbursed, not whoever
  submitted the receipt, and a card charge is not a reimbursement.
  [Decision — *Policy: Financial Policy, Art. IX §IX.2*]

- Whoever named the person reimbursed does not sign as Treasurer on that
  expense. The policy does not ask for this.
  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- A card charge is Everyday when its total is under $500 and no single item
  costs more than $250. An item is one unit, so three $100 items are three items
  under the limit. Both amounts are settings: finance may lower them, and only
  the Board raises them above $500 and $250.
  [Decision — *Policy: Procurement Policy, Art. IV §IV.1; Financial Policy, Art. XII §XII.4*; owner]

- A purchase from a single-source vendor is never Everyday.
  [Decision — owner]

- Finance may mark any card charge as not Everyday. The mark is permanent: it
  only ever adds the need for Board authorization.
  [Decision — owner]

- A card charge that is not Everyday reaches the books only once the Treasurer
  records what the Board authorized, in words. A blank record does not count,
  and until it is made the charge is treated as not authorized. Once recorded,
  it is not changed.
  [Decision — *Policy: Financial Policy, Art. XII §XII.4*; *Principle: fail closed*]

- Recording the Board's authorization is the Treasurer's act, under the
  Treasurer conflict rule: a finance holder without a conflict records it, and
  if there is none, a Board member without a conflict.
  [Decision — owner]
