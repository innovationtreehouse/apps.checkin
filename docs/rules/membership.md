# Membership

Application, background-check review, activation, membership fee, and renewal.

---

## Policy

### What membership is

- Membership belongs to a family, not an individual: everyone gains membership
  by being part of a member family, and the fee is per family per membership
  year. — *Membership Policy, Art. III §III.1*

- A member family is one household containing no more than two adults, with all
  youth being their children or dependents. — *Definitions Policy, Art. III,
  "Member Family"*

- The membership year runs 1 September to 31 August. One boundary applies to the
  whole organisation — membership is not a per-household anniversary running
  twelve months from the day a family joined. — *Definitions Policy, Art. III,
  "Membership Year"*

- A payment for a membership year may therefore buy less than twelve months, as
  when a family joins partway through one. — *Membership Policy, Art. XI*

- A family agrees in writing to the current membership agreement, separately for
  each membership year — a prior signature never carries over. Participation is
  not open to a family that has not. — *Membership Policy, Art. III §III.1*

- Members are responsible for keeping their information current within a
  membership year, including family composition, contact details, insurance, and
  emergency contacts. — *Membership Policy, Art. III §III.1*

- Nobody who has been dismissed from membership, or whose background check comes
  back without clear recognition of the individual, may be a member.
  — *Membership Policy, Art. IX*

### Deciding a matter you have an interest in

- A conflict of interest exists whenever someone is in a position to approve or
  influence an action that could benefit or harm themselves, their family by
  kinship — spouse, parents, guardians, children, siblings, grandparents,
  grandchildren, in-laws — or even a close friend. The appearance of one counts
  as much as the fact. — *Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.2*

- Someone with a conflict in a matter refrains from participating in any decision
  on that matter. A board member abstains from the vote; any other leader stays
  out of the decision entirely. There is no exception for seniority or for
  holding a privileged role. — *Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*

- A conflict is disclosed to the board in writing, before the person discharges
  any duty touching the matter. — *Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.3*

- The board may void any action taken by someone who participated despite a
  conflict. — *Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*

- The same person does not authorise, execute, and monitor a transaction.
  — *Financial Policy, Art. III*

### Background checks

- At least one adult in each family is checked; so is any other family member 18
  or over who will be present regularly. Third-party checks are not accepted.
  — *Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.1*

- Every volunteer 18 or older is checked. The obligation attaches to the role,
  not only to the household. — *Volunteer Policy, Art. IV*

- No entry to a location, event, or program until the check completes. A family
  mid-application may still attend as a hosted visitor. — *Membership Policy,
  Art. VI §VI.1*

- A check is valid for 29 months, after which a new one is performed at the next
  renewal. The board may check more often, or shift the timing to line up with
  membership years. — *Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.1*

- Reports are ordered, reviewed, and processed by at least two individuals who
  are independent of one another — not related — who have signed the key
  volunteer agreement and either serve on the board or are authorised in writing
  by it. — *Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.2*

- Two clearances therefore means two distinct people. One reviewer cannot supply
  both, so the same person may not review an application twice, and a second
  reviewer related to the first does not count either. — *Membership Policy,
  Art. VI §VI.2*

- Every reviewer has signed the key volunteer agreement. — *Membership Policy,
  Art. VI §VI.2*

- The check itself and any criminal-history detail stay between the person
  checked, the authorised reviewers, and the board. — *Membership Policy,
  Art. VI §VI.2*

- A check may result in approval, a restriction on how the person may volunteer,
  or denial. — *Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.2*

- Where the board treats an offence as disqualifying, objective supporting
  documentation is gathered, consistent with the obligation to document a
  denial. — *Membership Policy, Art. IX*

- A disqualified applicant receives the rights summary, a letter of
  disqualification, and a copy of the results, and may appeal in writing to a
  three-person committee kept as independent as possible from the original
  reviewers. — *Membership Policy, Art. VI §§VI.3–VI.4*

### Membership fee, refunds, and plans

- The board sets the cost of all memberships. The fee is a fee, not a charitable
  donation. — *Membership Policy, Art. XI*

- The board may discount the fee for financial need, college students, partner
  organisations, participants solely in service programs, and volunteers with no
  youth participating in any sponsored program. — *Membership Policy, Art. XI*

- The fee is refunded if the family is denied membership. An accepted offset for
  the cost of the background check is not refunded even then. Otherwise no
  refund is provided once membership is approved, absent an extreme circumstance
  approved by the board. — *Membership Policy, Art. XII*

- A membership payment plan does not extend beyond the membership year it
  covers. Late payment under a plan can lead to dismissal from membership.
  — *Membership Policy, Art. XIII*

### Member data

- Member information is shared on a need-to-know basis and is not sold.
  — *Records Policy, Art. IV; Art. V §V.1*

- A member may ask for inaccurate information to be corrected. — *Records
  Policy, Art. V §V.3*

- A member may ask for personal information to be deleted, except where it is
  legally required, needed for member safety, or a condition of the membership
  or program participation they wish to keep. — *Records Policy, Art. V §V.4*

## Assumptions

Things the app takes as true because they are handled outside it. They sit
between the two tiers because the Policy rules above rest on them and the
Procedure below is built on them.

- Every board member is a designated background-check reviewer. Board membership
  alone therefore qualifies someone wherever a reviewer is required — the queue,
  the attestation, and the sensitive fields that go with it. That they have signed
  the key volunteer agreement is procedure, under Key Volunteers below.

- A person grandfathered as a Key Volunteer when the agreement gate went live had
  signed the key volunteer agreement on paper before then; the record dated at
  that migration stands for that signature.

- A non-board reviewer was authorised in writing by the board before the reviewer
  role was granted. Granting the role is what represents that authorisation, so
  the assumption holds as long as granting it stays a deliberate act.

- Payment-plan terms are agreed within the limits policy sets. The app records
  that a plan was certified, not its schedule.

- Refunds are handled outside the app, including the one owed on denial of
  membership.

- Agreements are signed through an outside e-signature service, and the legal
  weight of the signature rests on it: identifying the signer, their consent to
  sign electronically, the audit trail, and keeping the signed copy. The app sends
  the agreement out and records who signed it and when.

- A background check is cleared within days of being performed, so dating the
  clearance rather than the check itself is immaterial against a window measured
  in months.

- The background-check vendor has no API. A check is started through a hosted
  consent link and its result comes back by hand. That is the arrangement, not a
  stopgap waiting to be automated.

- A check that comes back with a restriction on how someone may volunteer is
  carried by the board, not the app. Reviewers record only approval or rejection,
  so a restricted volunteer is approved here and the restriction lives in the
  board's own arrangement with them. The assumption holds as long as the reviewers
  who read the report are the people who set that arrangement.

- Declining a scholarship or payment-plan request is a conversation between two
  people. Telling the family is the board's own act and happens outside the app.

- Both signing adults in a household are marked as household leads. The app
  records no relationship between members, so lead status and age are together
  what separate a spouse from an adult child. A household running with one lead
  and an unmarked spouse is indistinguishable from one with an adult child, and
  marking the spouse a lead is what restores the distinction.

## Procedure

### Application and review

- An application in background-check review is the reviewers' work, not the
  board's. Board queues count blocked applications only.  [Decision]

- A check review is about one person, named when the check is marked started;
  the reviewer confirms the report is that person's and asks the Board for a
  wrong-person reset when it is not. A review that names nobody — one returned by
  that reset — is named by whoever decides first, approving or rejecting, as the
  one household lead whose report they read. A later decision may repeat that
  person and never names another.  [Decision]

- Naming a person who already has a check review of their own is refused when
  that review has a decision on it, is blocked, or still carries a rejection; the
  reviewer reviews them there. A review of theirs with no decisions is closed in
  favour of the new one.  [Decision]

- A reviewer who believes the named person is wrong asks the Board for a
  wrong-person reset, which returns the review to no person. The person cleared
  off it keeps any rejection it held, and naming them again brings it back.  [Decision]

- A person's check is disputed while a review of theirs is blocked, or while its
  rejection has been reset by the Board or changed to approve and the review has
  not cleared since. Undoing the changed approval does not end the dispute. A
  Board reset of a review that was never rejected disputes nobody.  [Decision]

- A household qualifies on its background check when at least one lead holds a
  valid clearance, nobody in the household is disputed, and no review requested
  for it is waiting to be named.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.1; Art. IX*]

- A rejection of anyone in the household blocks its open application; approvals
  do not outweigh it. A Board reset or the rejector's change to approve lifts the
  block and the application resumes, but it cannot clear while that person is
  disputed.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. IX*]

- The Board may release a household's application from the rejection of someone
  who has since left the household, with a written reason. The release is refused
  while anyone in the household holds a blocked review, for a denied household,
  and for an actor in the released household, in the departed person's current
  household, or who is that person. The application resumes where a reset of the
  blocking review would have put it. The rejection stays on the person and blocks
  any household they are in, this one included if they return.  [Decision]

- Every check-review rejection sends the Board one email. A rejection that blocks
  a paid application sends the paid-application email; any other names the
  person, their household and the review. A retry, a later settle or the daily
  sweep does not send it again; a rejection after a Board reset is a new decision
  and does.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

- Once a lead is cleared, a review the household asked for that still names
  nobody is closed and the application clears, unless that review still carries
  a rejection.  [Decision]

- A check review that still carries a rejection cannot be closed; it needs a
  decision from two new reviewers.  [Decision]

- A household has one open application at a time, joining or renewing. An
  active household only renews; a revoked one continues the renewal it already
  has open, or else starts a new joining application.  [Decision]

- A household with a blocked application cannot start another one. The board's
  "Reset for re-review" sends it back for two new approvals. Denying the
  household is a household action and leaves the application blocked; neither a
  reset nor the rejector's change to approve, below, reopens it then.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

- Nobody clears a blocked application on their own. After a rejection the check
  needs two approvals again, from two independent reviewers.  [Decision — Board ruling B5/B25]

- A person merge is refused while either record is disputed and the merge would
  carry in a newer check date or move a second check review onto that person.
  Otherwise the newer date is carried and both households are settled on their
  people's checks, whoever runs the merge, so a carried-in valid check can
  complete an application waiting on it.  [Decision]

- The reviewer who rejected may change their answer to approve. That starts the
  review over: every earlier decision on it, theirs and anyone else's, is
  discarded, and their approval is the first of the two it needs. The review
  keeps its person, who stays disputed until it clears.  [Decision — Board ruling B5/B25]

- A background-check date comes only from a clearance, or from a person merge
  carrying one forward. Nobody can change it in the app, earlier, later or by
  removing it; a wrong date is corrected by hand in the database.  [Decision — Board ruling B5/B25]

- The application's audit row and the merge archive are enough record of where a
  merged background-check date came from. No link to the original application is
  kept.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- The background-check result is never stored. The record is that reviewers saw
  it and what they judged — stricter than the policy, which permits the board to
  hold it.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.2*]

- A clearance is dated when the second reviewer clears it, and the validity
  window runs from that date.  [Decision]

- A reviewer may undo their own approval, with a written reason, while the review
  still waits on its second reviewer. The review returns to waiting for two
  approvals and keeps its person. Once the
  check has cleared, or the application is blocked or closed, the approval
  stands. A rejection is not undone; its reviewer may only change it to an
  approval, as above. A mistaken rejection goes to the Board's reset; see the
  review-volume assumption in `principles.md`.  [Decision]

- The duplicate-attestation cleanup is the Board's alone. When a person merge
  finds the same review recorded under two identities, only a board member may
  remove the duplicate.  [Decision]

- Removing a duplicate attestation reopens nothing. A check it helped clear stays
  cleared, with its date, and so does any application that clearance completed.
  A board member re-reviews such a check by hand.  [Decision — deliberate limit; owner ruling]

- The Participants directory is for sysadmins, the board and operations. A
  background-check reviewer works from the review queue.  [Decision — owner; *Principle: least privilege*]

- Every background-check decision that refuses, blocks or denies, every Board
  reset of a check review, and every payment certification, records a written
  reason — wider than the policy, which
  requires documentation only for a disqualifying offence. An approval needs
  none.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. IX*]

- The board may close an unpaid application ("Close application"), at any open
  step but blocked, older steps included; there is no minimum wait. It is never
  destroyed, and "Reopen" returns it to the step it was closed from. A paid,
  blocked or active application cannot be closed: a paid one only once its
  refund is recorded. The one exception is a restore, below, which also closes a
  blocked application. Reopening refuses a denied household, or one with another
  open application. The word "archive" is reserved for archiving a household.  [Decision —
  *Principle: decisions are reversible*]

- Archiving a household closes its open initial and renewal applications, blocked
  ones included, in the same step, as a restore does. Per-person applications stay
  open. Unarchiving the household reopens none of them.  [Decision — owner]

- A refund made in the online store is recorded by the board ("Record refund"),
  with a reason, by someone outside the household, on a paid application at any
  step that never became a membership. It stays offered until it is recorded,
  including after a denial. The application keeps its step, unpaid, and the
  background check is untouched; a paid application waiting on its background
  check returns to awaiting payment. A blocked application stays blocked: a
  refund never unblocks it or erases the rejection.  [Decision]

- Pre-designating a household volunteer-only sets what it owes and nothing else
  — it does not skip review or open payment. The policy grants only the
  discounting power; the limit is ours.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. XI*]

### Approving your own family

- Shared household is the app's test for a conflict — a workable proxy for the
  kinship, friendship and appearance tests policy states. Passing it is not a
  finding that no conflict exists.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- No role is exempt from the bar, however senior. A case whose only eligible
  deciders are all in the subject's household cannot be decided until the board
  finds someone who is not.  [Decision — *Policy: Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

- A reviewer is barred from a check review about someone in their household and
  from one their household requested. The Board's reset and release carry the
  same bar.  [Decision — *Policy: Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

- A board member is barred from a trusted-adult review their household
  requested, one where they are the trusted adult, and one whose trusted adult is
  in their household, whichever family asked. The adult counts as in the
  household when their linked person is, or, with no linked person, when their
  email or phone matches someone in it. A name alone never counts: a review
  whose adult has only a name is not blocked, and the board page warns that no
  conflict can be confirmed.  [Decision — *Policy: Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

### Activation

- Nothing advances toward payment until the agreement is signed and the
  background check is handled — either a lead's still-valid clearance, or a
  lead's check marked started, or a review the household asked for still waiting
  to be named. A check started for any other adult does not advance it.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. III §III.1; Art. VI §VI.1*]

- From there payment and review run in parallel, and membership activates on
  whichever finishes last. Neither alone activates.  [Decision]

- A payment arriving while anyone in the household is disputed is recorded and
  held; the membership does not activate.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. IX*]

- A board grant completes the payment step on an application already under way,
  joining or renewing. It stamps no other gate.  [Decision]

- Only an application completing — paid and background check cleared — makes a
  household a member. A board grant is money only: it pays the
  household's application under way, or opens one for the family to complete,
  and never sets membership directly or skips the check. A household already
  active stays active.  [Decision]

- An active household stops being one only by revocation or denial. A denied
  household never becomes active again by any route; the board's restore
  returns it to no membership, and the family starts over. The restore closes
  the household's open applications, blocked ones included, so nothing decided
  before or during the denial carries across. It is refused while one of them
  has a payment recorded, until the board records the refund.  [Decision]

- Anything that reaches a denied household after the denial — a family's
  payment, an approval that would clear its check, a start or renewal, a board
  grant or a household move — changes nothing and goes to the board's
  payment-problems queue with an email. Any other board action on a denied
  household is refused and tells the person acting why.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

- Denying a household revokes trusted-adult access in the same act, both ways;
  `people-households.md` (Trusted adults) states it. Nothing else the household
  holds is removed by the denial. Each program enrollment, pending or active, and
  each program role, volunteer or lead, held by one of its members in a program
  not yet finished goes to the board's payment-problems queue for removal by hand,
  so the board can speak to the program's leaders first. The board gets one email
  per denial that leaves it something to remove.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

- Revoking a household's membership does none of this: its trusted adults, its
  members' trusted-adult authorisations and its program ties are left as they
  are, and nothing goes to the board.  [Decision]

- A denied household is sent no outreach. Denial is checked again as each
  message goes out.  [Decision]

- One household lead signs the membership agreement, and the record names who
  signed. While one lead's signing request is open, another lead cannot start a
  second; the board resets signing, with a reason, which withdraws the open
  request. A signed agreement cannot be reset.  [Decision — superuser scope
  unsettled]

- Deny, restore, grant and revoke are each refused for the acting person's own
  household, and each applies only if the household is still in the status the
  action was decided against.  [Decision — *Policy: Ethics Policy, Art. III §III.5*]

- A denied household holds no authority anywhere in the app.  [Decision — *Principle: identity is not authorisation*]

- Denial locks every member of the household out of sign-in. Revocation does not
  — a former member keeps app access and loses facility privileges. Because the
  lockout reaches the whole household, denial is confirmed before it executes.  [Decision — *Principle: accountability*]

- A household containing a board member cannot be denied. The board role is
  removed in the app first, and that removal is a separate act from the denial.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- The denied landing page says only that access is denied. No reason, no contact,
  no mention of membership, no way to sign out.  [Decision — deliberate limit; *Principle: no existence oracle*]

- A household may have no membership. Facts only a membership carries are then
  absent, not zero.  [Decision — *Principle: fail closed*]

### Membership fee

- The membership fee has two rates, standard and Volunteer Family. The designation changes
  what a household pays and nothing about its review.  [Decision]

- A reviewer's Volunteer Family tick is written onto the household that requested
  the review, at once, as its own audited change; the review never stores it. A
  reviewer can set it and cannot remove it; removing it is a Board edit.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- The tick is offered, and written, only on a review of one of that household's
  leads. A review of any other adult in it, an adult child's or a non-lead's own,
  carries no tick.  [Decision — owner]

- A volunteer designation matches on canonical email, so address variants cannot
  change what a family owes.  [Decision]

- A volunteer designation applies only to a person who has signed in with that
  address. The Board sees everyone on file with the address when it designates
  it.  [Decision — *Principle: fail closed*]

- The board can activate a household with no payment by certifying a payment
  plan. Only the board certifies a payment; a sysadmin cannot.
  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. XIII*]

- A board grant records the membership year the Board chooses: the year in
  progress, or, inside the renewal window, the coming year. Each grant control
  names the year it records.  [Decision]

- A membership order the app cannot apply settles nothing and is raised for the
  board: a second order on an application already paid, one arriving after the
  board certified or closed the application or before payment was due, and one
  without the membership item. A store test order never settles a
  membership.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

- A refunded order never activates a membership; the app stops and raises it to
  the Board.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

- A board member declines a membership scholarship or payment-plan request in the
  app, and the decline sends nothing: the request clears and the household returns
  to owing its membership fee by the normal route. The decision is recorded; carrying it to
  the family is not the app's job.  [Decision — deliberate limit; *Principle: people decide about people*]

- A payment-plan request is decided once: an approval and a denial racing each
  other cannot both take effect.  [Decision]

- A household that has settled its membership fee but is still waiting on its background
  check, and is not denied, is a member for program pricing and for reaching
  members-only programs, and for nothing else. Mailing audiences, outreach, the store's member list,
  people search and payment-plan eligibility all continue to ask whether the
  membership is active. The narrowness is the rule: widening the shared question
  would hand every other benefit to a household that has not cleared.  [Decision]

- A background check that is later rejected does not unwind a program place
  already bought at the member price. The money is taken and the place is held;
  the board settles it with the family.  [Decision — *Principle: people decide about people*]

### Renewal

- A renewal needs a new background check unless a lead's is still valid at the
  boundary and nobody in the household is disputed. Where the validity length is unset, every background check counts as
  expired, so no clearance counts as fresh, and the compliance view reports
  nothing stale.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.1*; *Principle: fail closed*]

- A membership stays active while its renewal is in progress.  [Decision]

- Renewals open only inside the renewal window, and opening skips every
  household already handled this cycle — renewed, granted the coming year,
  joined in-window, or closed by the board — so no family is billed twice. The
  one exception is a board grant to a lapsed active
  household: that opens its renewal at any time of year, unless the household
  has already paid for the year now in effect.  [Decision]

- The board's "open renewals" button opens nothing while the coming year has no
  membership product to pay through. A board grant still opens one household's
  renewal, because the board pays it by grant once it reaches payment.
  [Decision — *Principle: an unconfigured setting does not act*]

- Renewals start only from the board's own actions: its "open renewals" button
  opens them for every household due, and a board grant opens one
  for that single household. No scheduled run opens them.  [Decision — *Board
  ruling B6*]

- Renewal outreach is sent only to household leads of households whose
  membership is active.  [Decision]

- The opening message goes to every such lead; the reminder goes only to those
  whose household has not completed renewal for the coming year.  [Decision]

- Coverage is what was bought, not what the calendar says. A household settled
  for the membership year now in effect — by joining, renewing, or a granted
  year — is covered to the boundary that closes the year; one that did not
  settle is covered only to the boundary that opened it, and reads as lapsed
  from that day. A membership with no membership fee recorded in the app has bought
  nothing it can see. Lapsing changes what the membership covers and nothing
  else: status does not move, and only a person revokes.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. III §III.1; Art. XI*; *Principle: people decide about people*]

- A membership payment or grant records the one membership year it buys, and
  coverage, the renewal sweep and member pricing read that recorded year. A
  checkout offered from the day the renewal window opens sells the coming year, to
  a family joining exactly as to one renewing; a background check clearing later
  never changes the year bought.  [Decision]

- A household is never told its background check has gone stale, because nothing
  acts on staleness between renewals. It is read inside a renewal, where it
  decides whether that renewal needs a fresh one.  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.1*]

- Membership intake requires a complete mailing address. Program registration
  deliberately does not.  [Decision]

- Treehouse accounts are not program families: no self-service member add, no
  membership grant. Only the member add is enforced server-side.  [Decision]

- Whether an application asked for a scholarship or payment plan, and which, is
  read by the board and finance only. A sysadmin or background-check reviewer
  works an application without it.  [Decision — owner: "payment plan and scholarship should be board and finance only. all staff shouldn't see it by default"]

### Compliance

- Nobody is removed for falling out of compliance. The view reports and sends
  nothing, so a violation waits until a person looks.  [Decision — deliberate limit; *Principle: people decide about people*]

- The compliance view lists everyone whose check is disputed, whatever the
  recheck window, and reports an active household with a disputed adult as stale
  even when a lead's check is valid.  [Decision]

### Adding a contact

- "Add contact", which creates a person from a name and email address alone, is
  for the Board and operations. The person gets a household of their own, with no
  membership, and leads it.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

### Background checks for individual adults

- Activating a membership opens a background-check obligation for that
  household's program-attached adults and for its signing adults. It is surfaced
  for follow-up and gates nothing — chasing it is a conversation, not a refusal
  at the door.  [Decision — deliberate limit; *Policy: Volunteer Policy, Art. IV*]

- The obligation follows an active membership. Leads of households that only ever
  started an application, were denied or revoked, or were created by an import owe
  nothing: they never became members, and the annual sweep must not open a check on
  someone who did not apply.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- The annual sweep judges age as of the membership-year boundary, which counts as
  inside it: turning 18 afterwards waits for the next sweep rather than firing on
  the birthday.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- Activating a new membership judges age as of the activation instead, so someone
  who has just turned 18 is caught on joining rather than read as a minor until
  the next boundary comes round.  [Decision]

- Under 18 is never checked, a minor being ineligible for one. This covers the
  young adult mentors, who volunteer without one.  [Decision — *Policy: Volunteer Policy, Art. IV*]

- No date of birth and no over-25 declaration means the age is unknown. Such a
  person is neither checked nor counted as covered; they are set aside for
  someone to resolve.  [Decision — *Principle: fail closed*]

- A check covers the adult who took it. One person's clearance never satisfies
  another's.  [Decision]

- "Check started" records that a check was started in the outside tool and puts
  that person's review in the reviewers' queue. It is not consent, which is given
  in the outside tool, and it approves nothing: membership still needs a lead's
  clearance by two reviewers.  [Decision]

- It is recorded only on the person whose check it is; a household's application
  never records it. A person may mark their own; a household lead may mark it for
  any adult in their household, at any time; the Board and sysadmins may mark it
  for anyone. A person known to be under 18 is never marked; one whose age is
  unknown may be. Neither a sign-in nor an email address is needed by the person
  marked.  [Decision — owner]

- Marking a check started has no own-household bar: it decides nothing. The bar
  sits on the approvals that clear it.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- A household lead sees, for each other adult in the household, only whether a
  check still needs marking — never their clearance, review or decisions.  [Decision — owner; *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.2*]

- A check review opened by mistake may be dismissed, with a written reason, while
  it carries no live decision: none was ever made, the approvals were undone, or
  a Board reset cleared them. A review that carries or was ever sent back for a
  rejection is never dismissed, nor is a blocked or cleared one. Dismissal undoes
  the mark and nothing else: it is not a decision, it approves, rejects, disputes
  and blocks nothing, and a check the person already owed stays owed.  [Decision — owner]

- Only a board member or a background-check reviewer may dismiss a check
  review, under the same household bar as any other act on a review. A person,
  a household lead and a sysadmin who is neither may not.  [Decision — owner]

- The audit names the person who marked it, and the person who dismissed it, so
  a family's act stays distinguishable from the Board's.  [Decision]

### Individual agreement for adult children

- An adult child of a household lead signs their own membership agreement rather
  than being covered by the household's. A spouse does not: a spouse stays on the
  household agreement.  [Decision]

- Signing it needs a sign-in of their own, and the lead supplies an email address
  for them.  [Decision]

- It is signed again every membership year, as the household agreement is.
  [Decision — *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. III §III.1*]

- Age for this obligation is judged as of the day it is opened, because it opens
  when the person turns 18 — deliberately unlike the background-check rule above,
  which judges age at the membership-year boundary. The two populations therefore
  differ for anyone with a birthday between today and the next boundary, and
  aligning them would either ask a minor to sign or leave a new adult uncovered
  for up to a year.  [Decision]

- Opened automatically only for a household member who is not a lead, is aged 18
  to 25 with a date of birth on file, and is in a member household and attached to
  a program that is running or ended within the last year. A non-lead adult over 25
  is left out: at that age, not being a lead reads as a spouse the household has not
  marked, not as an adult child.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- The one-year window is what stops the request repeating. An attachment is never
  cleared when a program ends, so without it someone who took a single class at 18
  would be asked again every year until they aged out. Every program carries an
  end date, so the window bounds them all.  [Decision — deliberate limit]

- Never opened for a household lead, who signs the household's agreement. An open
  individual agreement on a lead would take the place of the household one in the
  signing flow and stall the household's activation or renewal.  [Decision]

- The board may open one by hand for someone the automatic rule leaves out — not
  attached to a program, or over 25 — but never for a lead, and never for someone
  whose age is unknown.  [Decision]

- It gates nothing. An unsigned individual agreement never blocks participation,
  check-in, or the household's own membership.  [Decision — deliberate limit;
  *Principle: people decide about people*]

### Key Volunteers

- BOARD and BG reviewer, the two roles the policy names, are granted only to a
  person who holds the Key Volunteer role and has signed the current version of the
  key volunteer agreement. The check is at the grant, for every actor, system paths
  included; it is not repeated when the role is used. A person merge carries a role
  the merged-away record already held, which is not a new grant.  [Decision —
  @thpr, 2026-10-30; *Policy: Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.2*]

- Finance is granted on the same terms as BOARD and BG reviewer.  [Decision —
  @thpr, 2026-10-31]

- The Board and Finance roles are granted only to a Key Volunteer who has signed
  the current agreement; the check is at the grant.  [Decision — @thpr,
  2026-11-15; *Policy: Donor Privacy and Rights Policy, Art. V §V.02*]

- Keyholder is not gated on the agreement.  [Decision — deliberate limit, @thpr,
  2026-10-31]

- Using a role is not gated on the agreement. A person who holds one of those roles
  keeps using it while a re-sign is outstanding, and the app does not block review
  or board action on an unsigned agreement. The agreement is confirmed offline,
  person to person, before anyone is given a reviewer or board role.  [Decision —
  deliberate limit, @thpr, 2026-10-30, against *Membership Policy, Art. VI §VI.2*]

- The Key Volunteer role is granted and removed by the Board alone. It cannot be
  removed while the person holds BOARD, BG reviewer or Finance; that role is
  removed first. It grants no access of its own: the Board grants it to whoever owes the
  agreement, and the app records the role, not the reason.  [Decision — @thpr,
  2026-10-30]

- Granting the role opens the person's agreement. It settles on their signature,
  through the same e-signature flow as the membership agreement, and the Board may
  record a paper signature by hand. A signing request is created only when the
  person first chooses to sign, since each request costs money.  [Decision —
  @thpr, 2026-10-30]

- On a Key Volunteer Agreement, recording the signature by hand and attaching a
  signing request are for a board member only; a sysadmin who is not on the Board
  cannot, outside the bootstrap case below. Resetting the signing is not limited
  to the Board.  [Decision — @thpr, 2026-11-08]

- While no board member exists, a sysadmin may grant and remove the Key Volunteer
  role and record its signature by hand, so a new installation can seat its first
  board.  [Decision — @thpr, 2026-11-08]

- Removing the Key Volunteer role while its agreement is open and unsigned closes
  that agreement and recalls its signing request, with an audit record. It is a
  Board act, or a sysadmin's while no board member exists; nothing removes the
  role automatically.  [Decision — @thpr, 2026-11-08]

- The agreement is signed once and signed again only when the Board marks it
  materially changed by starting a new version. A new version asks every Key
  Volunteer to sign again, closes any agreement still open at an older version, and
  removes no one's role. A wording fix that is not material leaves the version
  alone.  [Decision — @thpr, 2026-10-30]

- Everyone who held BOARD, BG reviewer or Finance when the gate went live was
  recorded as a Key Volunteer who had signed version 1, on the date of that migration, without a
  signing request.  [Decision — @thpr, 2026-10-30]
